Your goals now say when they'll bloom.

Each goal looks at what you've put toward it over the last three full
months and, at that pace, names the month it gets there: "At this pace,
Japan trip blooms in May." Underneath it says what that pace is, so you
can see where the month comes from.

If you've set a target date, it tells you whether that pace gets you
there -- "On pace for your April date" -- or what about a month would:
"About $390 a month would bloom it by April." A target date that has
already gone by is simply set aside; the goal just shows its month.

A debt goal that follows a card does the same with the card's balance:
how much it's come down each month, and when, at that pace, it's paid off.

It needs a little history first. A goal you've added to in fewer than two
of the last three months says so ("Add to it a few more times...") instead
of guessing, and a card needs a few months of balances in Sorrel.

The month is an estimate from your own history, not a plan you set. It
moves when your pace does.
